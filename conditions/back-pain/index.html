Overview

Back pain is very common and, in most cases, is not caused by anything serious. It often improves with time, movement, and appropriate guidance.

Common symptoms

People describe symptoms in different ways. Commonly reported experiences include:

  • Aching or stiffness in the lower or upper back
  • Pain that worsens with certain positions
  • Difficulty bending or sitting for long periods
  • Muscle tightness or spasm

This list is general and does not confirm any specific diagnosis.

Common causes and contributing factors

  • Muscle or ligament strain
  • Prolonged or awkward postures
  • Sudden lifting or twisting
  • Reduced activity or general deconditioning

When to seek urgent medical care

Most back pain improves with time and appropriate care, but some warning signs need urgent medical attention.

  • Sudden loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the groin or inner thighs.
  • Progressive weakness, numbness, or loss of coordination in the arms or legs.
  • Severe pain after a significant fall, accident, or trauma.
  • Fever, unexplained weight loss, or night pain alongside your symptoms.
  • Chest pain, severe headache, difficulty speaking, or facial drooping — call local emergency services immediately.
